The question is what the property was worth then

A retrospective appraisal asks what the property was worth as of a historical date—not what it is worth today. The analysis therefore relies on market conditions and evidence relevant to that prior effective date.

Historical property condition matters

Current inspection observations can be useful, but the property may have been different on the effective date. Historical photos, permits, prior listings, renovation records and reliable client-provided information can help establish what existed at that time.

What we need

  • property address
  • exact historical effective date
  • intended use and intended users
  • property-condition information as of the historical date, when available
  • access arrangements for the current inspection when required
Assignment complexity

The availability and quality of historical property-condition information can affect the assumptions, scope, fee and report development.

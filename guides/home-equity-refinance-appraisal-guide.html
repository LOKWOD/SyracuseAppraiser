Home-Equity and Refinance Appraisal Guide

A refinance or home-equity appraisal is ordered for a lender's credit decision. The homeowner helps most by providing access and accurate property information—not by supplying a target value.

Updated August 17, 2026. General appraisal information only; not legal, tax, lending, engineering or accounting advice.

The lender controls the order

The lender or its authorized process selects the appraiser, identifies intended users and defines reporting requirements. A homeowner generally cannot substitute a privately ordered report without approval.

Loan amount, underwriting and borrowing capacity are lender decisions. The appraiser does not approve the loan.

Prepare access and documents

Make required rooms, basement, mechanical areas, garage, outbuildings and site features accessible. Tell the lender and appraiser about scheduling or occupancy restrictions.

Prepare a concise list of major improvements and approximate dates, with plans or permits when they clarify additions and finished areas.

Resolve description conflicts

Public records may omit additions, finished basements, accessory buildings or changes in use. Identify discrepancies factually.

Second kitchens, accessory units and multi-family use may require additional analysis. The appraiser must report observable characteristics rather than adopt a preferred label.

Keep value and underwriting separate

A credible appraisal can be followed by lender conditions involving title, insurance, eligibility or repairs. A value above the requested amount does not guarantee approval.

The lender controls report delivery and communication. Questions should normally be routed through that process.

Use reconsideration appropriately

If material facts or relevant market evidence were missed, provide concise documentation through the lender's formal channel.

A reconsideration permits review but does not promise a changed result. Avoid pressure, target values and long lists of weak sales.

Planning checklist

  • Confirm lender appraisal product
  • Make required areas accessible
  • Prepare improvement list
  • Provide plans for additions
  • Identify finished areas
  • Explain record discrepancies
  • Avoid target-value pressure
  • Route questions through lender
  • Use formal reconsideration process
  • Separate lending and valuation roles

Frequently asked questions

Can I choose the appraiser?

Lender independence requirements generally control selection.

Will renovations return full cost?

Not necessarily. Market contribution can differ from construction cost.

Why did the lender request repairs?

Eligibility and underwriting requirements are separate from value. Ask the lender which rule applies.

Can I provide comparable sales?

You may provide relevant information, but the appraiser independently selects and analyzes evidence.
